# Owner Operations Management

## Purpose

Owner Operations Management (OOM) is one of the core operating domains of the Construction Operations Platform.

It defines how owners manage facilities, acceptance rules, incentives, capacity, operational state, and recovery workflows across the platform.

## 1. Relationship to Platform Vision

Owner Operations Management supports all six Platform Pillars:

- Construction Operations: keeps facilities aligned with real field workflows.
- Intelligent Location Network: provides the acceptance and operational data needed to match drivers to locations.
- Driver Operations: gives drivers clear destination fit, status, and instructions.
- Owner Operations Management: is the primary owner-facing operating domain.
- Business Intelligence: turns owner actions into measurable operational insight.
- Financial Services: supports tips, fees, incentives, and future financial workflows.

OOM is not a standalone administrative layer. It is the operational control surface for owner-managed construction recovery locations.

## 2. Owner Operational Lifecycle

### Before Opening

Owners decide:

- whether the facility is open
- which materials are accepted
- whether capacity is available
- what tips or fees apply
- whether special instructions or restrictions are active
- whether the location is appropriate for current conditions

### During Operations

Owners decide:

- whether to accept or reject a driver or load
- whether capacity has changed
- whether tipping or fees should change
- whether the site should remain open
- whether mixed loads are allowed
- whether additional instructions are needed

### End of Day

Owners decide:

- whether to close the facility
- whether capacity should be reset or updated
- whether operational notes need to be recorded
- whether any outstanding driver visits or recovery items need follow-up

### End of Month

Owners decide:

- how the facility performed
- whether accepted materials should change
- whether tips or fees should be adjusted
- whether new capacity or rules are needed
- whether analytics indicate opportunities or gaps

## 3. Operational Events

Operational events are business events, not merely database updates.

Examples include:

- Facility opened
- Facility closed
- Capacity changed
- Material accepted
- Material rejected
- Tip changed
- Fee changed
- Driver arrival
- Driver check-in
- Driver check-out
- Recovery completed
- Washout completed
- Reward generated
- Repeat driver visit
- Owner profile updated

These events should create value for owners, drivers, administrators, and the platform.

Future OOM capabilities may include:

- Dynamic pricing
- Appointment scheduling

- Scale integrations
- Camera / LPR
- AI recommendations
- Predictive capacity
- Environmental reporting
- ESG reporting
- Contractor agreements
- Regional recovery networks

## 5. Definition of Success

Owner Operations Management is successful when owners can confidently operate their locations, attract the right traffic, manage acceptance and incentives, and understand how their sites contribute to platform value.

Measurable success may include:

- more qualified driver visits
- fewer rejected arrivals
- better capacity utilization
- improved acceptance clarity
- stronger repeat driver activity
- higher incentive effectiveness
- better operational visibility
- stronger analytics for business planning

## 6. Guardrails

- Do not treat owner operations as only a configuration screen.
- Do not reduce operational events to hidden database updates.
- Do not blur the line between facility state and reporting state.
- Do not introduce pricing or matching logic without explicit product definition.
- Keep OOM extensible for future recovery and network features.
