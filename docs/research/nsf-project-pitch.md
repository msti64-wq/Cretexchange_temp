# CreteXchange NSF Project Pitch

**Status:** Preliminary Draft for Program-Fit and Expert Review
**Research Status:** Proposed; no research results or production models are claimed.

## Project Concept

CreteXchange proposes to investigate whether heterogeneous evidence from construction-material activity can be transformed into confidence-scored records that improve recovery decisions while communicating uncertainty.

The technical concept combines:

- evidence-based confidence scoring
- construction-material classification
- constraint-aware material and destination matching
- geospatial recovery recommendations
- fraud and duplicate detection
- environmental measurement with explicit uncertainty
- controlled field-pilot validation

## Problem

Construction-material recovery decisions may depend on incomplete, inconsistent, or self-reported information about material identity, quantity, origin, destination, facility acceptance, evidence, and outcome. Proximity alone may not identify a compatible destination, and unqualified records may not support defensible marketplace or environmental decisions.

The prevalence, cost, and decision impact of these limitations are research assumptions requiring validation.

## Proposed Technical Innovation

The proposed R&D would investigate a linked technical system that:

1. represents heterogeneous transaction evidence and provenance;
2. produces explainable confidence scores rather than binary certainty claims;
3. classifies materials with uncertainty and review paths;
4. matches material and facility constraints alongside geography;
5. detects possible fraud, duplicates, and anomalies without treating flags as proof;
6. propagates confidence and uncertainty into environmental measurements.

## Why This Is Not Ordinary Marketplace Development

Ordinary marketplace development would add listings, search, accounts, payments, dashboards, or workflow automation using known software patterns. The proposed research instead asks whether uncertain, heterogeneous evidence can be combined and validated to produce reliable decision records, matching results, anomaly signals, and qualified environmental measures.

The technical uncertainty lies in representation, calibration, generalization, constraint reasoning, false-positive management, uncertainty propagation, and field validity. A marketplace interface alone does not resolve those questions.

## Research Objectives

- define a defensible evidence and confidence representation
- compare confidence scoring with simpler rule-based baselines
- develop and test construction-material classification approaches
- evaluate constraint-aware geospatial matching against proximity-only recommendations
- test duplicate, anomaly, and fraud signals with explicit false-positive costs
- determine whether environmental measures can communicate uncertainty without overstating outcomes
- validate feasibility in a bounded, governed field pilot

## Expected Research Outputs

- evidence and provenance model
- preliminary confidence-scoring method and calibration report
- research material taxonomy
- constraint and matching specification
- anomaly and duplicate evaluation
- environmental uncertainty framework
- pilot protocol and validation report
- commercialization and follow-on research decision

These are proposed deliverables, not existing artifacts or promised scientific outcomes.

## Commercial and Public-Benefit Hypothesis

If validated, confidence-scored records could improve participant trust, reduce incompatible recommendations and duplicate activity, support future recovered-material transactions, and make reporting more defensible. Potential beneficiaries include facilities, buyers, sellers, contractors, municipalities, agencies, and research partners. Willingness to pay and public benefit require separate validation.

## Phase I Fit Questions

- Is the technical uncertainty sufficient and appropriately scoped for the target NSF program?
- Can a six-month project produce meaningful evidence without building a production platform?
- Are appropriate investigators, datasets, facilities, and pilot participants available?
- Can privacy, consent, data ownership, licensing, and publication requirements be satisfied?
- Which simple baselines and measurable success criteria would make results credible?

## Current Platform Boundary

CreteXchange currently operates concrete washout-centered workflows. No evidence-confidence model, research material classifier, constraint-aware recommendation engine, predictive fraud model, or environmental-impact engine is claimed as implemented.
