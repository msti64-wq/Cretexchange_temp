# CreteXchange Research Advisory Board Plan

**Status:** Proposed; Board Not Yet Established

## Purpose

The advisory board would provide independent, multidisciplinary guidance on research quality, field validity, environmental methodology, data governance, commercialization assumptions, public benefit, and partnership readiness.

It would advise but would not replace company governance, principal-investigator authority, legal review, institutional review, participant consent, architecture approval, or Product Decisions.

## Proposed Expertise

- ready-mix, washout, recycling, and construction field operations
- construction and demolition material processing
- recovered-material buyers and contractor procurement
- material science and classification
- geospatial analysis, transportation, and routing
- statistics, machine learning, calibration, and model risk
- fraud, anomaly, and transaction verification
- lifecycle, environmental, and circular-economy measurement
- privacy, data governance, cybersecurity, ownership, and licensing
- municipal, regional, state, or federal program experience
- research commercialization, grants, and industry partnerships

## Composition Principles

- balance academic, operational, public, and commercial perspectives
- include people affected by field burden and data use
- document independence, conflicts, sponsorships, and confidentiality
- avoid overreliance on a single industry segment, customer, funder, or methodology
- compensate members transparently where applicable

## Recruitment Process

1. Define the first research program and expertise gaps.
2. Create role descriptions, expected time, term, compensation, and conflict requirements.
3. Screen candidates for expertise, independence, availability, and relevant conflicts.
4. Execute confidentiality, IP, publication, data-access, and conduct agreements as needed.
5. Record appointment, term, recusals, and decision boundaries.

## Proposed Operating Cadence

- quarterly program review
- additional protocol review before major grant submission or field pilot
- written pre-read with questions, methods, evidence, and risks
- minutes capturing recommendations, disagreements, conflicts, recusals, and decisions
- annual composition and effectiveness review

## Initial Deliverables

- review NSF pitch and Phase I hypotheses
- review evidence/confidence definitions and validation baselines
- review pilot scope and participant burden
- review environmental boundaries and uncertainty plan
- review data governance, consent, ownership, and publication terms
- review commercialization assumptions and partner strategy
- identify independent validation and university collaboration options

## Guardrails

- Advisory endorsement must not be presented as scientific validation.
- Members must not receive unrestricted participant or company data.
- Sponsor influence, conflicts, and dissenting technical views must be recorded.
- Public claims require approved evidence and methodology regardless of advisory support.
- `Project Atlas` remains provisional and is not adopted through advisory-board materials.
