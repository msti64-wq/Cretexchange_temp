# CreteXchange Market Analysis

**Status:** Strategic Market Hypotheses Requiring Validation
**Boundary:** This document contains no unsupported market-size estimate, forecast, or claim of current product availability.

## Market Problem Hypothesis

Construction-material recovery involves fragmented participants, local facility constraints, inconsistent discovery, limited visibility into material availability and acceptance, variable evidence quality, and disconnected operational records. These conditions may increase search and coordination effort, weaken reuse opportunities, and limit defensible regional insight.

The frequency, cost, and severity of each problem must be validated by participant, material, and region.

## Initial Market Wedge

The current wedge is concrete washout-centered driver-to-facility discovery and verified load drops. It gives CreteXchange a focused environment in which to validate participation, transaction trust, facility value, driver experience, and the $5-per-verified-drop launch economics.

Driver rewards and current transaction workflows remain the immediate product focus. The wedge should not be displaced by speculative marketplace or intelligence work.

## Customer Evidence Required

- frequency and cost of the current problem
- existing workaround and switching cost
- identity of user, beneficiary, purchaser, and approver
- measurable outcome customers care about
- willingness to participate and willingness to pay
- acceptable workflow and data burden
- minimum network density and geographic coverage
- trust, verification, dispute, and support expectations
- integration, procurement, legal, and security requirements
- effect of pricing on driver, facility, buyer, and seller participation

## Market-Development Sequence

1. Validate driver and facility participation in the concrete washout launch.
2. Measure verified transaction quality, repeat use, support cost, and facility value.
3. Validate material recovery supply, acceptance, and buyer demand by category and region.
4. Test matching and marketplace hypotheses before building broad commerce.
5. Validate enterprise reporting and integration value with defined buyers.
6. Validate regional and government use cases with aggregated, privacy-governed methods.
7. Explore benchmarking only after coverage and methodology are representative.

## TAM, SAM, and SOM Research Plan

TAM, SAM, and SOM are not estimated in this document. A defensible analysis should:

- define the unit of market activity and avoid double counting
- use dated, cited primary or authoritative sources
- segment by geography, material, participant, workflow, and revenue mechanism
- distinguish transaction volume from addressable platform revenue
- apply adoption, eligibility, product readiness, and participation constraints
- show assumptions, sensitivity ranges, exclusions, and source limitations
- receive finance and market-review approval before external use

## Strategic Risks

- a local network may not generalize across regions or materials
- facilities may value inbound activity but reject advanced subscriptions
- buyers and sellers may transact through established relationships off-platform
- verification cost may exceed transaction economics
- public-sector needs may be project-specific with long procurement cycles
- data may remain too sparse or biased for reliable intelligence
- participation friction or aggressive monetization may weaken network effects

## Validation Outputs

Market research should produce interview evidence, segment definitions, quantified workflow pain, payer maps, adoption blockers, pricing tests, regional density requirements, competitive alternatives, and explicit go/revise/stop decisions.
