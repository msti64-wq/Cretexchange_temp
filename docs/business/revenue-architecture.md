# CreteXchange Revenue Architecture

**Status:** Canonical Monetization Reference

**Authority Boundary:** Guides monetization choices but does not override CTX-ARCH-001, authorize pricing, or claim future revenue exists.

## 1. Purpose

This document evaluates how CreteXchange may capture a reasonable share of measurable value while preserving participation, trust, transaction quality, marketplace liquidity, and data governance.

## 2. Pricing Philosophy

- Keep participation friction low for drivers and other network-value generators.
- Charge parties receiving measurable operational, commercial, procurement, reporting, analytical, marketplace, or planning value.
- Avoid revenue mechanisms that reduce network liquidity or trust.
- Match pricing to evidence of value and customer willingness to pay.
- Separate transaction, subscription, marketplace, referral, and intelligence revenue.
- Do not sell confidential company-specific information without authorization.
- Apply privacy, consent, ownership, licensing, and environmental-claim controls to data-related revenue.
- Treat all pricing beyond the current approved foundation as a hypothesis until validated and approved.

## 3. Revenue Stream Framework

### 3.1 Verified Transaction Revenue

- **Customer:** Facility, owner, or other party designated by approved transaction economics.
- **Value delivered:** Discovery, verified completion, duplicate prevention, owner confirmation, evidence, and dispute controls.
- **Revenue mechanism:** Current $5 platform fee per verified drop; future fixed or percentage fees require separate approval.
- **Pricing approach:** Simple, transparent per-transaction pricing tied to a verified event.
- **Current status:** **Current foundation.** Exact production application remains governed by CTX-ARCH-001, CTX-ARCH-005, Product Decisions, and implemented rules.
- **Dependencies:** Sufficient participation, verified lifecycle, canonical billing, owner confirmation, support, and reconciliation.
- **Risks:** Fee sensitivity, verification cost, disputes, incorrect billing, or reduced participation.
- **Participation friction:** Drivers should generally face little or no direct participation charge.
- **Data governance:** Store only evidence and transaction data required for operations, audit, and approved analytics.
- **Required product maturity:** Production-grade verification, billing, reconciliation, and support.

### 3.2 Facility Subscriptions

- **Customer:** Washout, recycling, recovery, and material-processing facilities.
- **Value delivered:** Multi-location management, capacity, inventory, analytics, reporting, alerts, automation, integrations, or priority tools.
- **Revenue mechanism:** Recurring subscription with capability or location tiers.
- **Pricing approach:** Based on measurable operational value, scale, locations, or advanced capability usage.
- **Current status:** **Near-term to future.** No subscription package is asserted as currently available.
- **Dependencies:** Validated facility demand, entitlements, billing architecture, support model, and clear free-versus-paid boundary.
- **Risks:** Charging before network value exists, confusing base participation with premium tools, or high support cost.
- **Participation friction:** Basic network participation should remain accessible; charge for advanced value.
- **Data governance:** Facility analytics must remain role-authorized and protect other participants' confidential information.
- **Required product maturity:** Stable facility operations, account hierarchy, reporting, and subscription controls.

### 3.3 Marketplace Commissions

- **Customer:** Material seller, buyer, or both, as approved for each transaction model.
- **Value delivered:** Qualified demand, transaction coordination, trusted counterparties, and completed material exchange.
- **Revenue mechanism:** Fixed or percentage commission on completed marketplace transactions.
- **Pricing approach:** Proportional to transaction value and services provided without discouraging liquidity.
- **Current status:** **Future.** Recovered-material commerce is not represented as currently implemented.
- **Dependencies:** Listings, identity, inventory, material definitions, buyer/seller workflows, evidence, disputes, settlement, and legal review.
- **Risks:** Off-platform circumvention, low liquidity, material-quality disputes, tax or settlement complexity.
- **Participation friction:** Listing and discovery should be easy enough to build supply and demand before aggressive monetization.
- **Data governance:** Protect pricing, counterparties, inventory, and contract information.
- **Required product maturity:** Trusted, liquid marketplace with measurable completed transactions.

### 3.4 Buyer Service Fees

- **Customer:** Recovered-material buyers, contractors, developers, and large material users.
- **Value delivered:** Search, availability, reservation, procurement support, matching, logistics, or transaction assurance.
- **Revenue mechanism:** Per-transaction fee, service fee, membership, or premium procurement feature.
- **Pricing approach:** Based on procurement time saved, supply access, or transaction support.
- **Current status:** **Future.** No buyer service is currently claimed.
- **Dependencies:** Qualified inventory, accurate availability, material specifications, reservation and transaction workflows.
- **Risks:** Duplicate fees, price opacity, weak supply, or unverified material quality.
- **Participation friction:** Avoid fees that suppress early buyer demand or hide total transaction cost.
- **Data governance:** Buyer intent and procurement data require confidentiality and role controls.
- **Required product maturity:** Reliable supply, matching, reservation, and buyer support.

### 3.5 Seller Listing and Promotion Fees

- **Customer:** Material sellers, facilities, and processors.
- **Value delivered:** Visibility, qualified leads, featured placement, and demand generation.
- **Revenue mechanism:** Premium listings, featured placement, qualified lead fees, or campaign packages.
- **Pricing approach:** Free or low-friction base listings with optional paid promotion after liquidity is demonstrated.
- **Current status:** **Near-term to future.** No paid listing product is currently claimed.
- **Dependencies:** Marketplace audience, ranking policy, listing quality, attribution, and disclosure controls.
- **Risks:** Pay-to-play results reducing trust, low lead quality, or unfair ranking.
- **Participation friction:** Preserve enough free supply to sustain marketplace liquidity.
- **Data governance:** Clearly label sponsored placement and protect seller performance data.
- **Required product maturity:** Meaningful marketplace traffic and measurable lead value.

### 3.6 Enterprise SaaS

- **Customer:** General contractors, concrete producers, developers, infrastructure companies, and large material users.
- **Value delivered:** Multi-project administration, material tracking, procurement intelligence, diversion/reuse reporting, controls, integrations, and custom reporting.
- **Revenue mechanism:** Annual or recurring subscription, usage tier, project tier, or enterprise agreement.
- **Pricing approach:** Based on organization size, projects, locations, integrations, governance, and measurable workflow value.
- **Current status:** **Future.** No enterprise product is represented as available today.
- **Dependencies:** Organization model, RBAC, security, support, SLAs, integrations, reporting, and enterprise contracting.
- **Risks:** Long sales cycles, customization burden, security expectations, and scope fragmentation.
- **Participation friction:** Enterprise monetization should strengthen the network without limiting ordinary driver/facility access.
- **Data governance:** Tenant isolation, contractual use limits, retention, export, and audit requirements.
- **Required product maturity:** Stable multi-organization architecture and validated enterprise workflows.

### 3.7 Government and Regional Service Agreements

- **Customer:** Cities, counties, councils of governments, public works, state/federal agencies, and economic-development organizations.
- **Value delivered:** Aggregated material-flow, diversion, infrastructure-gap, capacity, participation, planning, or policy-support intelligence.
- **Revenue mechanism:** Annual service agreement, project contract, custom study, reporting contract, or grant-funded engagement.
- **Pricing approach:** Scope-, geography-, methodology-, and service-based contracting.
- **Current status:** **Future.** No government intelligence service is represented as currently available.
- **Dependencies:** Aggregated coverage, methodology, procurement readiness, legal/privacy review, accessibility, and reporting governance.
- **Risks:** Long procurement cycles, incomplete coverage, political misuse, or overstatement.
- **Participation friction:** Public products should not expose or burden individual contributors unnecessarily.
- **Data governance:** Primarily aggregated/anonymized outputs; no confidential company-specific disclosure without authorization.
- **Required product maturity:** Representative data, transparent methodology, governance, and public-sector delivery capability.

### 3.8 Premium Intelligence Products

- **Customer:** Facilities, buyers, sellers, enterprises, associations, and approved public stakeholders.
- **Value delivered:** Forecasting, pricing trends, capacity outlook, logistics optimization, matching, anomaly detection, or scenario analysis.
- **Revenue mechanism:** Subscription, report purchase, premium module, usage fee, or contracted analysis.
- **Pricing approach:** Based on decision value, coverage, freshness, methodology, and service level.
- **Current status:** **Future.** Existing operational metrics do not constitute a commercial intelligence product.
- **Dependencies:** Verified data, sufficient coverage, explainable models, evaluation, monitoring, and customer validation.
- **Risks:** Biased or unreliable outputs, false precision, unsupported environmental claims, or automation harm.
- **Participation friction:** Intelligence monetization should not make basic network participation harder.
- **Data governance:** Label verified, reported, estimated, and modeled outputs; protect confidential inputs.
- **Required product maturity:** High-quality longitudinal data and validated decision usefulness.

### 3.9 API and Integration Subscriptions

- **Customer:** Enterprises, technology partners, accounting/ERP/GIS providers, and approved agencies.
- **Value delivered:** Workflow integration, data exchange, automation, reduced duplicate entry, and ecosystem connectivity.
- **Revenue mechanism:** Subscription, usage tier, integration license, implementation fee, or support agreement.
- **Pricing approach:** Based on usage, endpoints, organizations, environments, support, and business value.
- **Current status:** **Future.** Internal APIs are not automatically commercial APIs.
- **Dependencies:** Versioning, authorization, rate limits, contracts, observability, developer support, and data entitlements.
- **Risks:** Security exposure, excessive support, breaking changes, or unauthorized redistribution.
- **Participation friction:** Integrations should simplify participant workflows rather than create lock-in barriers.
- **Data governance:** Contractual scopes, least privilege, consent, purpose limitation, retention, and audit logs.
- **Required product maturity:** Stable domain APIs and enterprise-grade security/operations.

### 3.10 Data Licensing

- **Customer:** Research organizations, universities, associations, enterprises, and approved public entities.
- **Value delivered:** Governed access to curated, documented, privacy-protected datasets.
- **Revenue mechanism:** Dataset license, research agreement, subscription, or limited-use contract.
- **Pricing approach:** Based on scope, uniqueness, permitted use, refresh, support, and exclusivity constraints.
- **Current status:** **Exploratory.** No data-licensing program is currently implemented.
- **Dependencies:** Consent, ownership terms, licensing rights, minimization, anonymization, retention, provenance, and legal review.
- **Risks:** Re-identification, loss of trust, unauthorized reuse, unclear ownership, or disclosure of confidential business data.
- **Participation friction:** Data licensing must not exploit participants or discourage network contribution.
- **Data governance:** Privacy, consent, ownership, purpose, access, retention, redistribution, and deletion controls are mandatory.
- **Required product maturity:** Mature governance, sufficient coverage, legal authority, and auditable delivery.

### 3.11 Sponsored Research and Industry Reports

- **Customer:** Associations, agencies, foundations, enterprises, and research partners.
- **Value delivered:** Qualified studies, industry reports, benchmarks, and collaborative research.
- **Revenue mechanism:** Sponsorship, grant, research contract, publication partnership, or commissioned study.
- **Pricing approach:** Project scope, methodology, data work, review, publication rights, and support.
- **Current status:** **Exploratory to future.** No sponsored research product is currently claimed.
- **Dependencies:** Research governance, methodology, conflict disclosures, publication controls, and qualified data.
- **Risks:** Sponsor influence, biased findings, unsupported claims, or unclear publication rights.
- **Participation friction:** Research should create ecosystem value and avoid participant burden or hidden use.
- **Data governance:** Document funding, methods, sources, limitations, privacy controls, and permitted use.
- **Required product maturity:** Credible data coverage and research-governance capability.

### 3.12 Financial-Service or Logistics Referrals

- **Customer:** Buyers, sellers, facilities, carriers, or service providers receiving a qualified referral.
- **Value delivered:** Access to payment, financing, insurance, hauling, routing, or logistics services.
- **Revenue mechanism:** Referral fee, revenue share, qualified lead fee, or partner agreement.
- **Pricing approach:** Transparent partner economics tied to delivered service and legal requirements.
- **Current status:** **Exploratory.** No referral model is authorized by this document.
- **Dependencies:** Legal/regulatory review, partner diligence, disclosures, consent, operational fit, and conflict management.
- **Risks:** Regulatory exposure, poor partner service, conflicts of interest, or pressure on neutral recommendations.
- **Participation friction:** Referrals should remain optional and should not distort trusted matching.
- **Data governance:** Share only authorized minimum data for the stated referral purpose.
- **Required product maturity:** Validated demand, approved partners, disclosures, monitoring, and support.

### 3.13 Professional Services

- **Customer:** Facilities, enterprises, regional organizations, agencies, associations, or research partners with a bounded need.
- **Value delivered:** Implementation support, configuration, integration, training, qualified analysis, custom study, or research assistance beyond standard product capabilities.
- **Revenue mechanism:** Fixed-scope project, time-and-materials engagement, retainer, training fee, or separately contracted study.
- **Pricing approach:** Based on documented scope, expertise, delivery effort, risk, and customer value rather than hidden product access fees.
- **Current status:** **Future.** No professional-services catalog is represented as currently available.
- **Dependencies:** Defined service boundaries, delivery capacity, contracts, acceptance criteria, insurance, support model, and conflict controls.
- **Risks:** One-off work fragmenting the platform, unclear deliverables, margin pressure, conflicts with research independence, or custom logic bypassing architecture.
- **Participation friction:** Services should reduce adoption burden without making basic network participation dependent on consulting.
- **Data governance:** Scope access to the minimum authorized data; define confidentiality, ownership, deliverables, retention, and permitted reuse.
- **Required product maturity:** Repeatable delivery methods, qualified personnel, governance, contracting, and a boundary between configurable platform work and bespoke services.

## 4. Monetization Decision Gate

Before approving any stream, document:

1. Primary customer and measurable value.
2. Paying party and willingness-to-pay evidence.
3. Revenue mechanism and pricing hypothesis.
4. Effect on participation, verification, and marketplace liquidity.
5. Product and architecture dependencies.
6. Financial, legal, privacy, licensing, and data-governance requirements.
7. Current, near-term, future, or exploratory status.
8. Validation plan and stop conditions.

No revenue stream proceeds to implementation without an approved Product Decision and applicable architecture updates.
